# Recording strip: decisions

- Beats come from transcript lines (a pause over 0.7 s or a sentence's end ends a line), clicks (`get_recording_activity`, cursor file), typing runs (key file), shortcuts (keyboard timeline as recorded), moments, and new pictures. Each keeps lead-in and settle time (`recording-beats.ts`); overlaps give way to the later beat, and gaps under 0.3 s go to the beat before. Lulls fill the rest.
- Picture activity is read from `<movie>-picture.jsonl` when the capture wrote it (`screenwide-activity`); recordings without it have no screen beats and no moving lulls.
- Clicks and typing have no label (the recording keeps none), so `BeatWhat` click/type labels are optional and read "Click" / "Typing".
- App switches are recorded without the app's name, so they are not beats.
- The edit is the only state: cuts are stretches no segment plays, jumps are segments that do not follow their predecessor in the recording, splits are joinable boundaries. Strip edits work by recorded stretches (`recording-strip-source.ts`) and rejoin the segments they cut where nothing holds them apart.
- Lull play is read from segment speed: the share of give most hurried lulls play with is the squeeze; a lull at speed 1 while others squeeze is kept; any other speed is retimed. With nothing squeezed every lull reads as squeezed, since the edit cannot tell a kept lull from one waiting to be.
- A beat plays at a speed of its own when its end is dragged with ⌥ (Alt) held (`beat-trim.tsx`); a plain drag still trims it, the opposite of a lull's end, since a beat is mostly trimmed and a lull mostly retimed (chosen 2026-11-22). Its speed is read from the segment under what plays of it, the squeeze never touches it, and what it saves counts as squeezed. Its island lists how it plays, as recorded or retimed, and choosing as recorded takes the retime back. A gap has no footage and is not retimed.
- A drag's steps are one undo step: the gesture ends 400 ms after the last step.
- Playback ranges are rounded to whole ms (`recording-timeline-playback.ts`), because native annotation/scene commands take integer ms and segment shares multiplied back out are not.
- The strip's side services (captions for the preview, camera background ranges, voice-note pause, overlay press) live in `use-recording-strip-services.ts`.
- Tracks are switched on the Tracks island, opened by the title bar's Tracks button (the last track still in cannot be switched off), and a recording's Copy Frame is in the title bar (⌘C too, as a screenshot's Copy). The preview plays as edited: the native player has no speed of its own beyond each segment's.
- Trim to a breath trims the lull's end to 0.3 s after it starts (`BREATH_S`, `strip-island-item.ts`), which plays it as recorded, as any lull trim does; moving picture inside it is not kept apart.
- Choosing a part on the strip (a beat, lull, moment, drawn range, annotation or scene), by pressing it, drawing it or scrubbing into it, leaves the playhead where it is (`stripChoice`, #83, user asked, 2026-11-21). It used to move the playhead to the part where it stood outside it, so the picture showed what was chosen, but that lost the place being worked on.
- The islands act on what is chosen: the Scene island and its asks on the scene chosen on the strip, where one alone is, wherever the playhead stands, else on the scene under the playhead (`inHand` in `use-recording-scenes.ts`, `ToolPanelScene.hasSceneInHand`). A layout chosen there changes that scene and adds none. The picture still shows the playhead's moment, and what is done on it (a drag, a pane's order, a crop) acts on the scene it shows (`ToolPanelScene.isAtPlayhead`), so a choice away from the playhead shows on the picture once the playhead is in it (user chose, 2026-11-21). Once a scene is chosen, the playhead moving onto another scene chooses that one, and moving off into a stretch with none keeps the choice (`useTimedSpanSelection`, user asked, 2026-11-21).
- An annotation moved while the playhead is in a scene takes a place of its own in that scene (`sceneShapes`, `scene_shape.rs` and its twin `recording-annotation-scenes.ts`): only its geometry (where it sits, its size, its turn, a lens's zoom) is the scene's, and its words, look and picture stay its own everywhere.
  - It sets off as the scene arrives, as the picture does, but on its own clock (`scene_shape/glide.rs`): a glide takes the scene's 600 ms for a short move and longer for a far one, as the square root of the distance against the picture's diagonal, up to 1.5 s, so a move across the picture is never a rush; it leaves early enough to be home as the scene ends, each glide kept to half the scene.
  - It glides only across an edge of the scene it shows across: one that appears inside the scene appears in its place there, and one gone before the scene ends goes from there (user asked, 2026-11-05). A scene that hands on to the next hands the annotation straight to its place there. Pinned annotations follow their content instead.
  - A cut that splits a scene gives both parts its places; deleting a scene drops them; the annotation's island says where it has one and resets it.
  - On the strip, its bar's line makes a short wave, a peak and a trough, over each glide, where the annotation is moving, and runs straight while it stands still (`annotation-bar-wavy-line.tsx`). The strip times the glides with a twin of the native clock (`recording-annotation-glides.ts`, whose test shares a case with `glide.rs`'s), at least 24 px wide so a quick move still reads. The whole line is one stroke, so a wave joins the straight line without a seam, and it is the line itself that changes, in its own colour and weight, so it reads apart from a pin's hatch and ticks. Rejected: a wave the length of a whole scene, which was odd to look at.
- ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows) snapping on the strip reads one list of edges (`stripSnapTargets`, `strip-snap.ts`): the beats', lulls', scenes', annotations' and cuts' starts and ends and the playhead, within 8 px (`edgeSnap`).
  - Every ⌘ drag uses it: a scene drawn on the rail (pressing or letting go of the key mid-drawing applies at once), a scene's end dragged or its body moved (its start, or else its end, settling; `scene-move.ts`, which keeps it between its neighbours), an annotation's bar moved or retimed; each leaves out the dragged item's own edges, every part of it, since they move with the drag.
  - Annotations and scenes are read in the parts the strip draws them in either side of a jump, and every end is measured where it is drawn, as the end of what stops there (`toX(end, true)`), so an end settles where the eye sees it. Snapped, the shared dashed guide (`SnapGuide`) shows: from a scene's end down through the whole strip, from an annotation's bar down past the lanes.
  - A beat's trim is not a ⌘ drag and keeps settling between words: a trim cuts what plays, and scenes and annotations are timed as they play, so their edges move with the cut and would not stay where the trim settled.
- The annotation lane shows only the rows its bars use, up to four (`MAX_SHOWN_LANES`); past that it scrolls inside that height (`ScrollArea`, an upright wheel scrolls it rather than the strip) and the rest of the strip stays put. A ⌘ snap's guide is drawn over the lanes, not in them, so scrolling never cuts it off. The annotation and shortcut lanes are left out when there is nothing in them, as the caption lane is.
- A lull's moving stretches each put a screen playing on its coil, but stretches whose glyphs would come closer than a glyph's room share one, in the middle of them, and where the glyphs would still take out more than half the coil, one stands in the middle of them all (`glyphPlaces`), so a lull full of motion still reads as a spring.
- A shortcut's keys never run past the strip's end, which would read as more recording there: keys pressed in the last moments end at the end, those before them drawn back to stay a gap clear, and every tick stays where its shortcut was pressed (`ShortcutLane`).
- A press or knob drag on the strip is one scrub, start at the press, moves as the pointer goes, end at the release (`useStripScrub`), so the frames between are rough and only the last is exact. The selection's outline stays on the picture throughout: a pane a scene places is outlined natively from the arrangement each frame is drawn in (`scene_selection.rs`), so it never trails the picture, and nothing hides it while scrubbing.
- Only the parts near the view are drawn: beats, lulls, captions and shortcuts reach a view's width past each side of it, and what is chosen or carried is drawn wherever it is (`strip-near.ts`). A 16-minute recording drew about 4,800 elements and took about 46 ms a frame to zoom in WebKit; drawn near the view, about 1,100 and 18 ms. A pan draws nothing new until the view comes within half a view of the edge of what is drawn. The scenes and annotation bars, fewer and stacked from all their neighbours, are drawn whole.
- Everything taken out of the recording is accounted for past the strip's end, like a storage chart: one band at the beats' height, as long as the recording's length less what plays now, in two segments (`TakenOutBand`, sized by `takenOut`). Deleted is the recorded length of the cuts (overlaps counted once); squeezed is the rest, what playing faster saves, so a lull retimed faster counts as squeezed along with the squeeze, and the strip and band together are always the recording's length. Squeezed sits first, against the end of what plays where the squeeze's handle is, so squeezing visibly fills the segment next to the handle while deleted stays put at the strip's end; deleted second. Squeezed is the existing hatch, deleted a flat `bg-fill` with the same dashed edge; each is captioned with its length (`editor-timeline-squeezed`, `editor-timeline-deleted`), and a caption that does not fit wraps out of sight. The minimap mirrors both past its content, in shares of the whole recording (deleted as a solid `bg-fill-strong` pill, the hatch for squeezed), so its content plus the band always spans its row.
- The squeeze's handle sits on the minimap at the end of what plays, where the band begins, and follows the pointer: a second is as wide in the minimap however much is squeezed (its room is the whole recording's), so the drag converts pixels to seconds at the minimap's own scale, and the minimap shortens under the handle while the hatch grows beyond it; dragged back out, the hatch shrinks until it is gone. The view may run on into the band (`clampView`'s `extent`), and a strip opened whole includes it, so neither squeezing nor cutting changes the strip's length.
- A change to what is taken out (a cut, a restore, an undo, the squeeze reset or a lull's play chosen) reshapes the strip as the screen switch does: the parts, the band's segments and the minimap glide together over `motionDurations.rearrange`. While a pointer is held anywhere in the window (`usePointerHeld`, read in the capture phase so a press acting on release counts as let go), the change is taken live instead, so dragging the squeeze handle or a trim follows the pointer. The glide is switched by a hidden cue drawn before the strip and minimap (`GlideCue`, a Tailwind `peer`) rather than a class on an element round them: React commits parents after children, and Chromium (WebView2) blurs a focused element synchronously as it is removed, as a restored seam or a cut beat is, which makes the browser take the parts' new places before a class round them could turn the glide on.
- With the screen's track out of the export (`enabledVideoTracks` without `primary`), the strip is found from the voice and the moments only (`withoutScreen`): clicks, typing, shortcuts and picture changes were read off the screen, so their beats, the shortcut lane, every screen thumbnail and lulls' moving stretches go, and lulls are the gaps between what is left. The edit is left alone: cuts are recorded time and stay, and a merged lull reads its play from its start until it is next squeezed or set. Camera frames are not put on speech beats.
- Switching the screen reshapes the strip as one rearrangement (`useStripReshape`): what keeps its id glides, new beats and lulls fade in where they land, and what went fades out where it was, over `motionDurations.rearrange`; the lanes collapse or grow as they do.
- The picture's native input view lies over the page, and WebKit drops the move that carries the pointer onto it, so a pointer carried straight from the strip onto the picture left the strip hovered (and later layout hovered whatever slid under that spot). The view's tracking area reports the pointer entering (`mouseEntered:`, `PointerPress::Arrived`, `preview://pointer-arrived`); the page marks the root `data-pointer-away` until its next `pointermove` (`usePointerAway`), and Tailwind's `hover` variant is redefined in `index.css` not to match while it is set, with a `pointer-away` variant hiding what the page draws at the pointer (the scene rail's ghost, the cut blade). Public API only; WebKit's `_simulateMouseMove:` would do it but is private. Windows has its own child window for the webview, whose mouse-leave reaches the page, so it sends nothing.
- A pinned annotation's bar shows a pin beside its tool's glyph in its badge (the line's gap under the badge widens to fit) and is named "… annotation, pinned to content".
  - Where it is hidden because its content is out of view, as the tracker found (`RecordingPinStatus.covered`) or the hand said (out-of-view keyframes, to the next keyframe or past the clip's end), the bar is hatched with the squeeze's hatch (`SQUEEZED_HATCH`), overlaps run together; each correction (a keyframe other than the pinned frame, `pinCorrections`) is a tick across the line.
  - Where the tracker is unsure of the content, having lost it (`weak`), found it off the frame (`hidden`) or under a cover (`under`), the bar's line is drawn in the warning colour over those stretches, run together (`AnnotationBarUnsure`): a solid mark, as the status colours are only ever used, not a tinted surface, so it reads apart from the hatch and through a chosen bar's line, and one colour for all three, since the island says which at the playhead.
  - Pins are in recording ms and the bar where it plays, so each stretch and tick is laid out through the strip's map, kept to the bar (`timelineAnnotationPin`, `recording-strip-pins.ts`).
- The strip's tools follow video editors: the blade (its tool, or ⌥ held) splits where it is pressed and draws nothing; the range tool draws a range, whose island shows its length and offers Cut (asking twice), and Delete cuts it too.
  - While a range is drawn the playhead follows the edge being drawn, so the picture shows the last frame it takes in, and stays there once let go. The range is drawn across every lane, from the knob's room to the captions, for the pointer to pass through; it is taken hold of in the row of beats and lulls, and drawn there while carried (`RangeBand`). The scenes lane is filled along its length so the empty rail reads as a lane.
  - The playhead's knob rides an unfilled band along the top (`strip-scrub-band.tsx`) that takes in the room under the picture too (the band's `-mt-separate`): a press anywhere there scrubs and keeps what is chosen, but for the range tool, which draws from there as well (user asked, 2026-11-04).
  - A GIF is made from Export alone: with the Export island out and GIF chosen (`isGifRange` in `export-island-store.ts`), the strip stays in focus and the range tool in hand over the locked strip; a range is drawn where none is, the GIF's longest (15 s) from the playhead, pulled back to end at the recording's end, and a range drawn then stops at 15 s of the edited recording (`useRangeCut`'s `limit`).
- The lanes over the timeline (scenes, annotations, songs and sound effects) are laid out and dragged in played seconds, as the edit times them (`strip-lane-time.ts`): a bar is one piece wherever it runs, across a jump or a gap, and a drag, a drawing or a snap moves it in time as it plays (user asked, 2026-11-14, after bars over a gap showed split at its edges while acting as one). What belongs to the footage (beats, lulls, cuts, captions, shortcuts) keeps recorded seconds, so a cut or a squeeze never moves it off what it was read from. Only while a move is dragged are the lanes drawn where their footage will play, in recorded seconds and in parts either side of each jump, as letting go splits them, so they glide with the footage.
- A gap is time inserted with nothing recorded behind it (#38), for a title card, a header or a pause: the transport's Insert gap, or G (user asked, 2026-11-14), puts 3 s at the playhead, splitting the recording there, and inside a gap grows it instead (`recording-strip-gaps.ts`).
  - In the edit it is a segment past the recording's end (a source of one or more), each gap in an hour-long slot of that empty source of its own, slot 0 never used, so no gap meets the recording or another gap and every moment of the timeline keeps a source moment of its own. Everything that maps between the recording and the timeline, annotations and scenes included, works on gaps unchanged; the native side reads a range past the recording's end as a gap (`TimelineRange::is_gap`).
  - Annotations and scenes are carried onto the timeline a gap changes by their footage, as for every edit, so one spanning where a gap goes is split around it. The songs and sound effects, which no edit re-times, move with what comes after the gap themselves; one already playing keeps playing through.
  - No scene plays over a gap (user chose, 2026-11-14, after a scene drawn across one showed as three pieces). Above a gap the scene rail is out of bounds, hatched with a dashed edge as squeezed time is, the pointer showing not-allowed (`scene-rail-gaps.tsx`, user asked, 2026-11-14): a press there draws nothing, and a scene drawn, stretched or moved towards it stops at its edge as at another scene's, a moved one whole. One that crosses a gap anyway, as a project saved before this opens, is cut back to either side of it, a side under a scene's least length (0.5 s) dropped (`recording-scene-gaps.ts`, run on every edit and on opening).
  - It is drawn among the beats, as a beat of its own kind, and its island chooses its background from the Frame island's tiles (`useBackgroundParts`), the recording's own until Clear gap background, and removes it, closing up its time; Delete removes it too. The empty source between slots is no cut, its edges no jump to put back, and its time adds to what plays without counting as taken out.
  - Chosen, its ends are dragged (`recording-strip-gap-edges.ts`; user reported the handles did nothing, 2026-11-14). Either end shortens or lengthens it, up to the longest gap, nothing of the recording cut, what follows it, songs and sound effects with it, moving by as much. Its start dragged moves the strip's view by as much too (`useStripView`'s `shiftSinceHold`), so the start stays under the pointer and what came before is pushed back (user asked, 2026-11-14, over the start rolling into the recording before it, which cut footage). The view is set from where it was when the start was taken hold of and how far the start has gone since, not step by step, so it does not drift from the pointer (user reported, 2026-11-14). With the view already at the strip's start it can go no further back, so a start drawn in there leaves its end to come in instead. Each end is a moment of the gap's own empty stretch, so a drag says where it is and the same drag always gives the same gap; a gap is placed a longest gap into its slot, so its start can be drawn earlier (one made before then cannot).
  - Dragged whole, it slides: its start follows the pointer in played time, the place it splits the recording moving with it, rather than being lifted and dropped between beats as a beat is (user asked, 2026-11-14; `gap-slide.ts`, `slideRecordingTimelineGap`). Each step takes it out where it was, the recording made one again there, and plays it from the moment the pointer is at, the recording split there, keeping its length and background, with what is drawn over it sliding with it and the songs and sound effects staying with the recording either side of both places. A press that travels less than 4 px chooses it instead. Gathered with beats, only the recording's stretches move.
  - The preview and the export draw a gap as a blank scene of its own, among the recording's scenes (`screenwide_scenes::with_gaps`, from `TimelinePlan::gap_scenes` and, in the preview, the gaps `use-scene-preview-sync.ts` sends): its background, the gap's own (kept on its segment) blooming in as a scene's does, and what is drawn over it; the cursor and the keys hidden, and silence. The screen and the camera fade out over the gap's first transition window and back in over its last, inside the gap as a scene's change is inside its clip (user asked, 2026-11-14), the picture held from before the gap fading both ways; a gap at either end of the video holds there.
  - A still in a gap, scrubbed or paused, decodes the frame playback holds there, where the recording was left before the gap (or, for a gap the video starts with, where it picks up after), not the recording's last frame (`held_ms`, `gap_preview.rs`). A seek lands on the frame that starts playing at its moment (`recordingTimelineOutputToSourceStart`): at a join, the stretch after it, so the first frame after a gap is the recording's, not the gap's own empty end (user reported a frame of the wrong picture ping as a scrub crossed a gap's end, 2026-11-14).
  - With the playhead in a gap, the screen's and the camera's chrome (outline, handles, rotation gizmo) is not drawn and neither pane can be picked, as for a pane a scene hides, since the gap shows neither (`scenePanePicks`'s `isInGap`, user asked, 2026-11-14).
