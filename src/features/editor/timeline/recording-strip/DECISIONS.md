# Recording strip: decisions

- Beats come from transcript lines (a pause over 0.7 s or a sentence's end ends a line), clicks (`get_recording_activity`, cursor file), typing runs (key file), shortcuts (keyboard timeline as recorded), moments, and new pictures. Each keeps lead-in and settle time (`recording-beats.ts`); overlaps give way to the later beat, and gaps under 0.3 s go to the beat before. Lulls fill the rest.
- Picture activity is read from `<movie>-picture.jsonl` when the capture wrote it (`screenwide-activity`); recordings without it have no screen beats and no moving lulls.
- Clicks and typing have no label (the recording keeps none), so `BeatWhat` click/type labels are optional and read "Click" / "Typing".
- App switches are recorded without the app's name, so they are not beats.
- The edit is the only state: cuts are stretches no segment plays, jumps are segments that do not follow their predecessor in the recording, splits are joinable boundaries. Strip edits work by recorded stretches (`recording-strip-source.ts`) and rejoin the segments they cut where nothing holds them apart.
- Lull play is read from segment speed: the share of give most hurried lulls play with is the squeeze; a lull at speed 1 while others squeeze is kept; any other speed is retimed. With nothing squeezed every lull reads as squeezed, since the edit cannot tell a kept lull from one waiting to be.
- A drag's steps are one undo step: the gesture ends 400 ms after the last step.
- Playback ranges are rounded to whole ms (`recording-timeline-playback.ts`), because native annotation/scene commands take integer ms and segment shares multiplied back out were not (this also affected the legacy footage move).
- The legacy band is deleted; its side services (captions for the preview, camera background ranges, voice-note pause, overlay press) moved into `use-recording-strip-services.ts`.
- Track toggles and Copy Frame, which lived only in the band, have new homes: a Tracks island opened by the transport's Tracks button (above the strip's tools; the last track still in cannot be switched off), and Copy Frame in the title bar for recordings (⌘C too, as a screenshot's Copy). Preview speed, also only in the band, is gone: the preview plays as edited, and the native player has no speed of its own beyond each segment's.
- Trim to a breath trims the lull's end to 0.3 s after it starts (`BREATH_S`, `strip-island-item.ts`), which plays it as recorded, as any lull trim does; moving picture inside it is not kept apart.
- Choosing a part on the strip (a beat, lull, moment, drawn range, annotation or scene) moves the playhead to its start only where the playhead stands outside it (`TimelineStrip`'s `choose`); inside it, the playhead stays. What Shift gathers, and shortcuts, never move it.
- A scene, chosen on the strip or drawn on its rail, brings the playhead (still only where it stands outside it) to the scene once arrived rather than to its start: its start plus its arrival, `TRANSITION_MS` (600 ms, `sceneLandingMs`, the twin of `arrange.rs`'s), so the picture shows the scene settled. A scene too short for two arrivals lands at its middle, and one starting the video, which arrives at once, at its start. The islands choose no scene of their own: the Scene island acts on the scene under the playhead.
- ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows) snapping on the strip reads one list of edges (`stripSnapTargets`, `strip-snap.ts`): the beats', lulls', scenes', annotations' and cuts' starts and ends and the playhead, within 8 px (`edgeSnap`). Every ⌘ drag uses it: a scene drawn on the rail (pressing or letting go of the key mid-drawing applies at once), a scene's end dragged, an annotation's bar moved or retimed; each leaves out the dragged item's own edges, every part of it, since they move with the drag. Annotations and scenes are read in the parts the strip draws them in either side of a jump, and every end is measured where it is drawn, as the end of what stops there (`toX(end, true)`), so an end settles where the eye sees it. Snapped, the shared dashed guide (`SnapGuide`) shows: from a scene's end down through the whole strip, from an annotation's bar down past the lanes. A beat's trim is not a ⌘ drag and keeps settling between words: a trim cuts what plays, and scenes and annotations are timed as they play, so their edges move with the cut and would not stay where the trim settled.
- The annotation lane shows only the rows its bars use, up to four (`MAX_SHOWN_LANES`); past that it scrolls inside that height (`ScrollArea`, an upright wheel scrolls it rather than the strip) and the rest of the strip stays put. A ⌘ snap's guide is drawn over the lanes, not in them, so scrolling never cuts it off. The annotation and shortcut lanes are left out when there is nothing in them, as the caption lane is.
- A press or knob drag on the strip is one scrub, start at the press, moves as the pointer goes, end at the release (`useStripScrub`), so the frames between are rough and only the last is exact. The selection's outline stays on the picture throughout: a pane a scene places is outlined natively from the arrangement each frame is drawn in (`scene_selection.rs`), so it never trails the picture, and nothing hides it while scrubbing.
- Everything taken out of the recording is accounted for past the strip's end, like a storage chart: one band at the beats' height, as long as the recording's length less what plays now, in two segments (`TakenOutBand`, sized by `takenOut`). Deleted is the recorded length of the cuts (overlaps counted once); squeezed is the rest, what playing faster saves, so a lull retimed faster counts as squeezed along with the squeeze, and the strip and band together are always the recording's length. Squeezed sits first, against the end of what plays where the squeeze's handle is, so squeezing visibly fills the segment next to the handle while deleted stays put at the strip's end; deleted second. Squeezed is the existing hatch, deleted a flat `bg-fill` with the same dashed edge; each is captioned with its length (`editor-timeline-squeezed`, `editor-timeline-deleted`), and a caption that does not fit wraps out of sight. The minimap mirrors both past its content, in shares of the whole recording (deleted as a solid `bg-fill-strong` pill, the hatch for squeezed), so its content plus the band always spans its row.
- The squeeze's handle sits on the minimap at the end of what plays, where the band begins, and follows the pointer: a second is as wide in the minimap however much is squeezed (its room is the whole recording's), so the drag converts pixels to seconds at the minimap's own scale, and the minimap shortens under the handle while the hatch grows beyond it; dragged back out, the hatch shrinks until it is gone. The view may run on into the band (`clampView`'s `extent`), and a strip opened whole includes it, so neither squeezing nor cutting changes the strip's length.
- A change to what is taken out (a cut, a restore, an undo, the squeeze reset or a lull's play chosen) reshapes the strip as the screen switch does: the parts, the band's segments and the minimap glide together over `motionDurations.rearrange`. While a pointer is held anywhere in the window (`usePointerHeld`, read in the capture phase so a press acting on release counts as let go), the change is taken live instead, so dragging the squeeze handle or a trim follows the pointer. The glide is switched by a hidden cue drawn before the strip and minimap (`GlideCue`, a Tailwind `peer`) rather than a class on an element round them: React commits parents after children, and Chromium (WebView2) blurs a focused element synchronously as it is removed, as a restored seam or a cut beat is, which makes the browser take the parts' new places before a class round them could turn the glide on.
- With the screen's track out of the export (`enabledVideoTracks` without `primary`), the strip is found from the voice and the moments only (`withoutScreen`): clicks, typing, shortcuts and picture changes were read off the screen, so their beats, the shortcut lane, every screen thumbnail and lulls' moving stretches go, and lulls are the gaps between what is left. The edit is left alone: cuts are recorded time and stay, and a merged lull reads its play from its start until it is next squeezed or set. Camera frames are not put on speech beats.
- Switching the screen reshapes the strip as one rearrangement (`useStripReshape`): what keeps its id glides, new beats and lulls fade in where they land, and what went fades out where it was, over `motionDurations.rearrange`; the lanes collapse or grow as they do.
- The picture's native input view lies over the page, and WebKit drops the move that carries the pointer onto it, so a pointer carried straight from the strip onto the picture left the strip hovered (and later layout hovered whatever slid under that spot). The view's tracking area reports the pointer entering (`mouseEntered:`, `PointerPress::Arrived`, `preview://pointer-arrived`); the page marks the root `data-pointer-away` until its next `pointermove` (`usePointerAway`), and Tailwind's `hover` variant is redefined in `index.css` not to match while it is set, with a `pointer-away` variant hiding what the page draws at the pointer (the scene rail's ghost, the cut blade). Public API only; WebKit's `_simulateMouseMove:` would do it but is private. Windows has its own child window for the webview, whose mouse-leave reaches the page, so it sends nothing.
- A pinned annotation's bar shows a pin beside its tool's glyph in its badge (the line's gap under the badge widens to fit) and is named "… annotation, pinned to content". Where it is hidden because its content is out of view, as the tracker found (`RecordingPinStatus.covered`) or the hand said (out-of-view keyframes, to the next keyframe or past the clip's end), the bar is hatched with the squeeze's hatch (`SQUEEZED_HATCH`), overlaps run together; each correction (a keyframe other than the pinned frame, `pinCorrections`) is a tick across the line. Where the tracker is unsure of the content, having lost it (`weak`), found it off the frame (`hidden`) or under a cover (`under`), the bar's line is drawn in the warning colour over those stretches, run together (`AnnotationBarUnsure`): a solid mark, as the status colours are only ever used, not a tinted surface, so it reads apart from the hatch and through a chosen bar's line, and one colour for all three, since the island says which at the playhead. Pins are in recording ms and the strip in recorded seconds, so the mapping is a division, kept to each part a jump splits the bar into (`timelineAnnotationPin`, `recording-strip-pins.ts`).
- The strip's tools follow video editors: the blade (its tool, or ⌥ held) splits where it is pressed and draws nothing; the range tool draws a range, whose island shows its length and offers Cut (asking twice), and Delete cuts it too. While a range is drawn the playhead follows the edge being drawn, so the picture shows the last frame it takes in, and stays there once let go. The range is drawn across every lane, from the knob's room to the captions, for the pointer to pass through; it is taken hold of in the row of beats and lulls, and drawn there while carried (`RangeBand`). The scenes lane is filled along its length so the empty rail reads as a lane. The playhead's knob rides an unfilled band along the top (`strip-scrub-band.tsx`) that takes in the room under the picture too (the band's `-mt-separate`): a press anywhere there scrubs and keeps what is chosen, but for the range tool, which draws from there as well (user asked, 2026-11-04). A GIF is made from Export alone: with the Export island out and GIF chosen (`isGifRange` in `export-island-store.ts`), the strip stays in focus and the range tool in hand over the locked strip; a range is drawn where none is, the GIF's longest (15 s) from the playhead, pulled back to end at the recording's end, and a range drawn then stops at 15 s of the edited recording (`useRangeCut`'s `limit`).
