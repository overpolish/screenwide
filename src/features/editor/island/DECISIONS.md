# Editor island decisions

Decisions for wiring the islands into the redesigned editor (`src/storybook/explorations/editor`). Where the exploration still behaves otherwise, these win.

## Choosing something takes up its tool

The Select tool has no island of its own. Choosing something takes up the tool it belongs to, and that tool's island shows it.

- **Annotations:** choosing one, on the picture or on the strip, takes up its pen. The toolbar shows that pen pressed, and the pen's island edits the chosen annotation alone.
- **The pen stays in hand:** each click or drag on the picture places another annotation, even while one is chosen, and the new one becomes the chosen one. There is no click to let the chosen one go first: placing several text boxes in a row should take one click each, as in Shottr.
- **Letting go:** pressing Select, pressing the pressed pen again, or Escape puts Select back in hand with nothing chosen.
- **The next annotation follows the last:** restyling the chosen annotation is also the pen's style from then on, so the next one placed looks the same. A pen taken up with nothing chosen shows that style, with no Delete: the annotation islands take `onDelete` as optional, and leave it out for that.
- **Several annotations chosen with the marquee:** the Marquee island says how many and deletes them all, asking twice. They are of different kinds as often as not, so it offers nothing of their look; one alone is shown on its own pen's island.
- **Scene clips:** choosing one takes up the Scene mode, and the Scene island shows that clip (`sceneIsland` with `scene` set).
- **Beats, pauses and ranges:** these have no tool, so Select stays in hand and their own islands show them.
- **Captions:** the Captions mode opens the Captions island, for recordings only: audio has no picture to show captions over, so it is not offered there. It is the Text island's look (template, faces, colours) with the captions' layout where a text's placement goes: one line of the type's size and, in leant letters, the key word's where the template sets one word apart; then the position grid with, stacked beside it, the line count over the most words a caption holds ("Max 8 words"). Each template sets its own max words and lines; the island lets them be changed. A layout option changed by hand is kept when another template is chosen, unless it is at the template's own value, which then follows the next template as an untouched one does (`handLayout`, applied as each change is made; the island's wiring uses it too). Under the faces, one line of what they say and how: hold until next, hide punctuation, hide fillers, censor profanity and all caps, each rule drawn as what it leaves out and swapped for its Lucide-style off twin while on. Profanity is a toggle; it is starred out in the style the settings keep, which the island no longer offers a choice of. Its row ends with Word by word and whether they show, as the Keyboard island's does; hidden, the section folds away. Reset to default and Set as default cover the look, the layout and the rules together, where the legacy panel had them per tab, and wrap within the island's width rather than widening it. Editing a caption's own text, breaks, hidden words and key words is the transcript's work, not the island's.
- **Script, the first pair of islands:** the transcript is edited as a whole script, never one caption at a time, which is slow. One Script island (`island/script/`) shows it as flowing paragraphs, the word being said tinted and kept in view; a click seeks, a double-click or Return corrects a word's spelling, which belongs to the transcript and so shows in every mode. The edit holds the word alone: its punctuation stays either side and is kept unless punctuation is typed on that side, and clearing its letters hides it from the captions, as Backspace does (`caption-word-parts.ts`). The Captions mode opens it beside the Captions island, marking where captions break, hidden words and key words, with the legacy caption editor's actions behind a right click or the menu key (break here, join, clear a break, hide or show, key word, restore spelling) and Backspace to hide or show; a right click on a break or a space asks only about that gap. Holding Command means the same in both modes, taking words out of what the mode makes: a Command-click hides a word from the captions or shows it again, and a Command-drag does so to every word it reaches, each left as the first one was made, previewed as it goes and made in one undo step (`useCaptionPaint`), as the Transcript mode removes and restores words from the video. Reset caption edits takes back every edit made in the script: what was placed by hand, and spelling, put back to what was heard in one batch of corrections (`correct-many`), since a reset that leaves a corrected word alone reads as broken. The right click opens the app's own menu in a panel window, as every menu does, so it works from an island as from a panel; a story outside the app has no panel to open, so try it in `pnpm dev:storybook-native`. The panel's caption editor and the island share the editing (`useCaptionWordEditing`) and the text view, so the two cannot drift apart. The two islands stand either side of the picture, the Captions island at the start and the Script island at the end, rather than where they were opened from (`EditorIslandPairStage` in stories). Tab or a shortcut moves focus between them; Escape, or choosing something else, closes both.
- **Transcript, the Script island on its own:** the toolbar has two modes for the script, Captions (the Captions and Script islands as a pair) and Transcript, which opens the Script island alone, at the editor's end, in place of the legacy Transcript panel, keeping all it did: words chosen by click or drag, removed and restored with Command held, dragged to move the footage, corrected in place, the word menu (remove, restore, edit, restore spelling, remove every one alike, not profanity, add or change a scene), and pauses as chips. The row is `[Script] … [search]`: the search field at its end, a fixed width so typing never resizes the island, and a Replace toggle that opens Replace with and Replace all at the top of the section, everything under it moving down with the black; the two halves meet in `script-search.ts`, since the row and the section are drawn apart. Under the words: the hint, then one line of quiet glyph chips that remove at once the pauses, the fillers ("um") and the profanity ("#@!"), each with how many it takes, its glyph struck through and what it would take shown red while hovered. Islands show no tooltips, so a chip's full name ("Remove 6 pauses") is for assistive technology alone. A chip shows only while it has something to take, and the line goes with the last; then Transcribe again and Restore all. Until there is a transcript, the section is the line the Captions island shows (`IslandTranscriptLine`). The legacy panel and the island share the editing (`useTranscriptEditing`) and the text view, so nothing is lost when the panel goes.

The exploration differs here: `select` in `model/use-editor.ts` keeps Select in hand, `place` puts Select back after placing, `updateMark` restyles the annotation without the pen, and `island/island-content.tsx` gives a chosen annotation and a chosen scene islands of their own, and gives a pen a Done button.

## The tool column

The editor's tools stand in one column at the workspace's start edge (`toolbar/editor-tool-column.tsx`): Select and the pens, a rule, then the modes, which open islands for the whole recording.

- **Every pen:** image, arrow, text, redact, draw, shape, highlight, counter, focus and magnify, most used first, as the legacy toolbar orders them. Spotlight is the focus pen's dimmed look, as on the annotate island, so it has no button.
- **A short window:** the column measures the room the modes leave, and the pens that do not fit go into a More menu at the end of the pens; the pen last taken from it, by the menu or its shortcut, takes the place before the menu's button (`annotationToolLayout`). The modes always show.
- **Modes are named and drawn as their islands:** Frame, Screen (Screenshot for a screenshot), Camera, Scene; Cursor, Keyboard; Captions, Transcript, Sound, each group set apart by a rule. Camera shows only where the recording has one. A screenshot offers Frame and Screenshot; audio, with no picture to show captions over, offers Transcript and Sound with no pens. The Cursor island's glyph is the column's pointer with its click, since Select already has the bare pointer.
- **Tooltips:** the column is a window toolbar, not an island, so its buttons name themselves and their letter in a tooltip.

## Where the Select panel's rows go

In the exploration, the screen, camera and audio tracks can't be chosen, so the legacy Select panel's rows move to the islands they belong with. The rule: a setting that holds for the whole recording sits on the island for its layer, and the Scene island keeps only what one scene sets over its stretch.

- **The Scene island keeps where each pane sits in a scene:** its layout, each pane's zoom and position, and the screen's rotation. With no scene in hand it frames the recording. A pane's corners and the camera following the face are its layer's, so Scene offers neither; nor a shadow, which a scene has none of. Legacy's Recenter for a followed face is Position's reset here: while the camera follows the face, the middle of its box is the face.
- **The Frame island is the canvas alone:** its size and background, and for a screenshot the canvas's corners (`backgroundRadiusPercent`). These are the finished picture's outer corners; the Screen island's are the captured picture's, inside the padding.
- **The Screen island, formerly Picture and before that Crop, holds the screen of a recording or a screenshot, and is titled Screen or Screenshot after what was captured:** its crop's shape on one line; on the next, the inset presets (None, Tight, Medium, Large, shares of the picture's shorter side), which extend the edges with the colour found behind the content, then the screen's own corners and its drop shadow, for the whole recording. Recenter sits beside Reset crop, putting what was captured back in the middle. The Crop tool and its shortcut open it.
- **A new Camera island holds the camera, for the whole recording:** its crop's shape, as Screen holds the screen's, with Reset crop at its foot on both; its effects (Enhance, Remove background, Blur or Replace drawn as icons with Replace at that line's end, and the objects picked in the matte), its corners and drop shadow, and whether it follows the face. It opens from a Camera mode in the toolbar's Modes group, as Cursor and Keyboard do, shown where the recording has a camera, and with it open the crop's handles on the picture are the camera's. It is not part of Scene, because these apply to the whole recording and a scene to one stretch.
- **Over a scene, the Screen and Camera islands' corners, and the Camera island's Follow face, are that scene's.** A scene can round the screen and the camera and have the camera follow the face its own way, and the whole recording's setting would visibly do nothing under it. So with the playhead in a scene (`isInScene`), those controls edit the scene's own (`RecordingSceneClip.radius`, `followFace`), and a note at the island's foot says they apply to this scene only. Everything else on those islands holds for the whole recording. These are the only controls for a scene's own, and a scene that has its own keeps it: nothing hands it back the recording's. Legacy set the recording's Follow face on the camera's Select panel and a scene's on the Scene panel; this puts both on one control.
- **The Sound island holds every track's volume and the microphone's tools.** It opens from a Sound mode in the toolbar, also offered for audio-only recordings. Each track's volume is a typed figure in decibels, named by the track, with its reset; under the microphone's, its tools on one line: Studio sound, Reduce noise, Vocal cleanup, Auto volume and, while Auto volume is on and the recording has system audio, Studio ducking, as the legacy Select panel offered them. Studio sound stands in for Reduce noise and Vocal cleanup while it is on. A tool whose model is missing offers its download in place of its toggle, as the Camera island's do. Remove silences stays with the pauses on the strip and with Transcript.

## When the legacy panels are removed

The Camera, Cursor, Frame, Keyboard and Sound islands still read copy from the legacy panels' file, `locales/en-US/editor-panels.ftl` (`editor-panels-*`), which the legacy Select, Cursor, Keyboard and Frame panels share with them. Deleting those panels must not delete that copy with them: move each message an island still uses into `editor-island.ftl` under an `editor-island-*` id, update the islands' `t()` calls, run `pnpm i18n:generate`, and only then delete what nothing reads. `grep -rn 'editor-panels-' src/features/editor/island` lists what is left to move.
