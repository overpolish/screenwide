# Editor island decisions

How the editor's islands, its tool column and the title bar's modes work together, and why.

## Choosing something takes up its tool

The Select tool has no island of its own. Choosing something takes up the tool it belongs to, and that tool's island shows it.

- **Annotations:** choosing one, on the picture or on the strip, takes up its pen. The toolbar shows that pen pressed, and the pen's island edits the chosen annotation alone.
- **The pen stays in hand:** each click or drag on the picture places another annotation, even while one is chosen, and the new one becomes the chosen one. There is no click to let the chosen one go first: placing several text boxes in a row should take one click each, as in Shottr.
- **Every pen takes hold of every annotation:** with any pen in hand, the pen for strokes included, an annotation of any kind under the pointer halos and shows the pointer; a click chooses it (taking up its own pen, as above), a drag carries it, and only a press on nothing drawn (or on the screen, camera or an image) draws. A stroke chosen this way stays chosen under the pen, whose island edits it: a chosen annotation is let go only when a pen for another kind is taken up (`toolDisagreesWithAnnotation`). A fresh stroke is still let go as it is drawn, so the next press draws again.
- **Holding ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows) holds Select:** with a pen in hand, the modifier going down with nothing pressed, in the page or on the picture, puts Select on the picture for as long as it is held (`useSelectHold`, `select-hold.ts`): its cursors, and a click or drag chooses or carries a layer, the camera, an image or an annotation. Only the cursor and the column's Select change; the island stays the pen's, and the layer's own chrome stays down until a press (`select_held` in the engine), so ⌘Z, ⌘D or ⌘C flash nothing. A press that takes hold of something (`PointerPress::Down { took }`, `preview://native-pointer`) puts Select in hand for good, so letting go leaves it there; an annotation chosen takes up its pen as ever. Let go with nothing taken, the pen comes back. Any other key with the modifier, or the window losing focus, ends the hold at once. The modifier that brings Select is spent on it: presses under it are neither the ⌘-click toggle nor a ⌘ snap (`shortcut_spent`), so a ⌘-click chooses the annotation alone. A ⌘ pressed mid-press keeps its meaning (snapping) and holds nothing.
- **Where the hold does not apply:** only pens hold Select. The marquee keeps ⌘ for its additive band, which needs ⌘ before the press; the modes (Frame, Scene) and the Crop tool draw their own chrome on the picture, which a hold would swap out: the crop shows the whole capture as a ghost under its handles, and Select held would take the ghost and the handles away and bring them back on every ⌘. The ⌘ snapping added for drawing scenes lives on the strip's scene rail, not the picture, so it is untouched. The Shortcuts pane lists only rebindable app shortcuts and shows no fixed editor ones (⌘D is not there either), so the hold is not listed; it belongs with the editor's other fixed keys if that pane ever gains them.
- **Letting go:** pressing Select, pressing the pressed pen again, or Escape puts Select back in hand with nothing chosen.
- **⌘D (Ctrl+D) lets go of everything chosen, in both editors:** annotations, the chosen layer or track, the open mode and the scenes with it, the strip's beats, lulls, ranges, moments, shortcuts and groups, captions and shortcut picks, Select back in hand. Unlike Escape it does not stop at whatever owns Escape first; it reaches every choice at once. The island window forwards it as it forwards the other keys; a text field keeps it. The Shortcuts pane lists only the app's global shortcuts, so it is not listed there.
- **A mode lets go of the annotations for good:** taking up a mode (Frame, Screen, Camera, Scene; a screenshot's Frame and Screenshot) or the Crop tool lets the chosen annotations go, and putting it away brings none back (`useSelectToolMemory` forgets them); only Select put down and taken up again, through pens or the marquee, gets them back. In a recording, putting a mode away by its button leaves nothing chosen at all, the track included, as Select does (`backToSelect`); in a screenshot, Crop and Frame put away leave the layer chosen, as they work on it, so the Screenshot mode stands open on it after them, and the Screenshot mode put away lets its layer go, so its island leaves.
- **Hand-drawn draws a fresh seed each time it is turned on:** every press that turns Hand-drawn on, on the Highlight or Shape island, redraws the stroke from a fresh seed (`applyStyle`), so turning it off and on again rerolls the wobble; a press while it is on turns it off.
- **The next annotation follows the last:** restyling the chosen annotation is also the pen's style from then on, so the next one placed looks the same. A pen taken up with nothing chosen shows that style, with no Delete: the annotation islands take `onDelete` as optional, and leave it out for that.
- **Several annotations chosen with the marquee:** the Marquee island says how many and deletes them all, asking twice. They are of different kinds as often as not, so it offers nothing of their look; one alone is shown on its own pen's island.
- **No Clear drawings:** the drawing pen has no action that takes every stroke away; the marquee chooses several and deletes them, as it does any annotations.
- **A layer chosen with Select opens its mode, in both editors:** Screen and Camera (a screenshot's Screenshot) are not an island over Select but Select on their layer.
  - **Opening:** however the layer comes to be chosen with Select in hand, by a click or drag on the picture, by its button, or by Select coming back over a layer still chosen, the mode's button is lit and its island out, exactly as opening it from the title bar does; on-picture handles never stand on a layer with no island beside them. The mode is worked out from the layer chosen (`columnMode` in `recording-column.ts` and `screenshot-column.ts`), so the button and the island cannot disagree with the handles.
  - **Switching and closing:** choosing the other layer switches modes; choosing nothing (a click on empty picture, which `up.rs` reports as a choice of nothing, while a drag from there still pans), Escape or ⌘D puts it away with the layer let go. Inside a scene too.
  - **With the island-only modes:** in a recording a chosen layer takes over an island-only mode (Cursor, Keyboard, Captions, Transcript, Sound), and opening one of those lets the layer go. Select stays in hand, so the layer is still moved by dragging it.
  - **With nothing chosen:** the Screenshot mode opened by its button takes the newest layer; the Crop tool crops the chosen layer, the newest where none is.
- **Focus and spotlight are one pen:** the Focus island's dim toggle turns a chosen focus into a spotlight and back (`annotationKind`), keeping its box and corners; with the pen in hand and nothing chosen it takes up the other tool instead. The column shows the focus pen pressed for either.
- **Scene clips:** choosing one takes up the Scene mode, and the Scene island shows that clip (`sceneIsland` with `scene` set). The chosen scenes belong to the mode: putting the mode away lets them go, and deleting the chosen scene, by Delete or the island, puts the mode away with Select back in hand (`useSceneModeChoice`).
- **Beats, pauses and ranges:** these have no tool, so Select stays in hand and their own islands show them.
- **Captions:** the Captions mode opens the Captions island, for recordings only: audio has no picture to show captions over, so it is not offered there. Editing a caption's own text, breaks, hidden words and key words is the transcript's work, not the island's.
  - **Look and layout:** it is the Text island's look (template, faces, colours) with the captions' layout where a text's placement goes: one line of the type's size and, in leant letters, the key word's where the template sets one word apart; then the position grid with, stacked beside it, the line count over the most words a caption holds ("Max 8 words"). Each template sets its own max words and lines; the island lets them be changed. A layout option changed by hand is kept when another template is chosen, unless it is at the template's own value, which then follows the next template as an untouched one does (`handLayout`, applied as each change is made; the island's wiring uses it too).
  - **The rules:** under the faces, one line of what they say and how: hold until next, hide punctuation, hide fillers, censor profanity and all caps, each rule drawn as what it leaves out and swapped for its Lucide-style off twin while on. Profanity is a toggle; it is starred out in the style the settings keep, which the island offers no choice of.
  - **The row and the defaults:** the row ends with Word by word and whether they show, as the Keyboard island's does; hidden, the section folds away. Reset to default and Set as default cover the look, the layout and the rules together, and wrap within the island's width rather than widening it.
- **Script, the first pair of islands:** the transcript is edited as a whole script, never one caption at a time, which is slow. One Script island (`island/script/`) shows it as flowing paragraphs, the word being said tinted and kept in view.
  - **Correcting a word:** a click seeks, a double-click or Return corrects a word's spelling, which belongs to the transcript and so shows in every mode. The edit holds the word alone: its punctuation stays either side and is kept unless punctuation is typed on that side, and clearing its letters hides it from the captions, as Backspace does (`caption-word-parts.ts`).
  - **With the captions:** the Captions mode opens it with the Captions island, marking where captions break, hidden words and key words, with the caption actions behind a right click or the menu key (break here, join, clear a break, hide or show, key word, restore spelling) and Backspace to hide or show; a right click on a break or a space asks only about that gap. The right click opens the app's own menu in a panel window, as every menu does, so it works from an island as from a panel; a story outside the app has no panel to open, so try it in `pnpm dev:storybook-native`. The editing lives in `useCaptionWordEditing`.
  - **Command held:** it means the same in both modes, taking words out of what the mode makes: a Command-click hides a word from the captions or shows it again, and a Command-drag does so to every word it reaches, each left as the first one was made, previewed as it goes and made in one undo step (`useCaptionPaint`), as the Transcript mode removes and restores words from the video.
  - **Reset caption edits:** takes back every edit made in the script: what was placed by hand, and spelling, put back to what was heard in one batch of corrections (`correct-many`), since a reset that leaves a corrected word alone reads as broken.
  - **Where the pair stands:** the two islands stand at the picture's opposite top corners, the Captions island in the end one as every mode's island is, and the Script island in the start one, their tops level: both are too tall to stack (`host/editor-island-panel.tsx`; `EditorIslandPairStage` in stories). Tab or a shortcut moves focus between them; Escape, or choosing something else, closes both.
- **Transcript, the Script island on its own:** the title bar has two modes for the script, Captions (the Captions and Script islands as a pair) and Transcript, which opens the Script island alone, in the picture's top start corner where it stands in the Captions mode too.
  - **What it does:** all a transcript editor does: words chosen by click or drag, removed and restored with Command held, dragged to move the footage, corrected in place, the word menu (remove, restore, edit, restore spelling, remove every one alike, not profanity, add or change a scene), and pauses as chips. The editing lives in `useTranscriptEditing`.
  - **The row:** `[Script] … [search]`: the search field at its end, a fixed width so typing never resizes the island, and a Replace toggle that opens Replace with and Replace all at the top of the section, everything under it moving down with the black; the two halves meet in `script-search.ts`, since the row and the section are drawn apart.
  - **Under the words:** the hint, then one line of quiet glyph chips that remove at once the pauses, the fillers ("um") and the profanity ("#@!"), each with how many it takes, its glyph struck through and what it would take shown red while hovered. Islands show no tooltips, so a chip's full name ("Remove 6 pauses") is for assistive technology alone. A chip shows only while it has something to take, and the line goes with the last; then Transcribe again and Restore all.
  - **Before a transcript:** the section is the line the Captions island shows (`IslandTranscriptLine`).

## Where the island lives

The picture is drawn natively under the page, and a native view over it takes every press, so an island in the page could be seen but not pressed. The island lives in the editor's own panel window (`tool-panel-<workspace>`), which is laid over the editor's whole content area under the title bar (picture, strip and columns) for every island, and follows it as the editor moves and is resized (`island/host/use-editor-island-window.ts`); the islands stand against the picture's rect inside it (`island/host/island-window-place.ts`, passed as `inset`), and follow it as the strip or a column folds. The window never changes as one island gives way to another: its frame and the island's place in it are two windows' worth of change, which never land on the same frame, so resizing it on a switch would flicker.

The window lets clicks through everywhere but the island's parts, which the island reports as it changes shape (`set_standalone_listbox_hit_area`), as the recording island's window does, so the picture, strip and columns under it hover, scroll and drag as ever. It reads what the editor publishes to its panels (`ToolPanelSnapshot`) and sends back the same asks (`ToolPanelPatch`), so the editor has one way to be read and changed.

**Every island opens in the picture's top end corner** (the top right, the top left in a right-to-left language), fixed, whatever brought it out: a pen, a mode, a chosen layer or annotation, the marquee's choice, Tracks. It is always found in one place, and never lines up with a button that may sit far from it. The exceptions are a strip item's island, which stands over the item (below), and the Script island, which stands in the top start corner (above).

## How the island changes what it holds

Everything the island holds comes, goes and changes over one way, on every island and at every level (the row, the line under it, the section, a line in the section, a group of controls on a line): `IslandParts` (`src/primitives/island/island-parts.tsx`). No island animates its parts on its own, with `AnimatePresence` or otherwise: each would move differently, and a part lifted out of the flow can slide from an edge the island is not anchored to. Glide's island is the exception: a black of its own drawn at the pointer, it follows a hand mid-gesture, so waiting out the three steps would leave the gesture over before it showed. It fades in quickly holding what it has to show, changes look at once with a short crossfade while its black springs to the new shape, and goes at once when the window is sent, without a check; the app's icon stands on its destination rather than riding there from the middle (user asked, 2026-11-04).

- **Out, then reshape, then in:** a part leaving fades where it stands, keeping its room, so nothing beside it moves while it can still be seen. Where something replaces it, the room changes hands at once, the black and the parts that stay glide to the new shape together, and the newcomers come into focus once the black has settled round them, never riding its moving edge. Where nothing replaces it, it folds its room away (both ways, so a part wider than the rest takes its extra width with it), and the parts after it close up in the flow; nothing is moved over another or out of a scroll area that clips it, which a glide of the parts that stay would do.
- **The black follows a fold exactly, along the fold's own axis:** while a part folds, the black takes each size as it comes instead of gliding after it (`island-follow.ts`); a glide started afresh every frame trailed the fold by over a hundred pixels. A glide already under way runs on to its end. Only the axis the parts are laid along follows: a column's part folds its height, so the black's height follows while its width keeps gliding. Following both, the black's side jumped: a part folding down narrows the section only to the width of what stays, which is the first sliver of the part's own width fold, over in a frame or two (a redaction switching from Fill to Erase lost 27 px in one frame).
- **A section that goes while the same thing stays in hand folds away like any part** (`use-held-section.ts`), so turning an option off that took the section away (Show for the keyboard, captions) fades the section and the row's part with it, then closes both as one. With something else in hand, the row and section change over together instead.
- **The gap between parts goes with the part after it:** the layout's gap is read once and laid as each part's margin, so a part folding away takes its gap with it rather than leaving it to close in a jump.
- **Coming out and going away are two steps, on every island** (`island-presence.ts`): out, the black grows empty and what it holds fades in where it stands once the black is nearly there (about 100 ms before it settles); away, what it holds fades out where it stands and then the empty black closes. Revealing the content with the growing black had it slide in from the black's moving edge. With nothing over it, the empty black's shape shows, so it is kept one shape: a section spanning the row rides the row's width for the whole of coming out (it trailed it as a narrower box, the row showing as a wider header with pointed lips), the section's black does not fade apart from the row's (a hard-edged band over a fainter section), and the top corners never outgrow a row smaller than them (points past its sides).
- **A corner island is placed from its own shape, never from the page** (`placed-island.tsx`): the island reports where its parts are from its own middle, worked out from its shape's values, and is placed from that. Placed from where its outline was measured on the page, a frame behind the values, it chased its own move, shifting left as it grew and right as it shrank before settling, and closed past its inset into the window's corner.

## A crop's shape

The Crop tool's shape (Free, Original, ratios) is a way of cropping, kept per layer for as long as the editor is open (`crop-aspect-store.ts`), not saved with the project. Choosing a ratio cuts the crop to the largest window of that shape inside the one it had, about its middle; the handles then keep that shape as they are pulled (`crop_keeps_shape`, the same resize a scene's box uses), and a press outside the crop draws no new one, which would be free of any shape. Reset crop gives the whole capture back, free.

**Over a scene, the screen's crop is that scene's own**, so different scenes can crop the screen differently while the recording's own crop holds outside every scene. A scene's crop is its screen's box and framing (`RecordingSceneClip.boxes.screen`, `screen`): the crop tool crops it in place, the picture staying where it sits and the window being the box, exactly as it crops a custom scene's box; Free draws, moves and pulls its edges freely, and a shape cuts it about its middle (`shapedCustomPane`).

A preset scene has no box of its own to crop, so the first crop that changes it makes it custom at the boxes it shows, which alone moves nothing (`customRecordingSceneClip`), as a full scene's first move does; a press that changes nothing leaves the preset. The shape is remembered per scene (`cropAspectKey("recording", "scene:<id>")`), so the island shows the shape of the scene under the playhead.

Reset crop over a scene shows the whole picture again: a preset's zoom goes, and a custom box grows back to the picture's shape about its middle, fitted and slid onto the canvas (`uncroppedCustomPane`). The note at the Crop island's foot says the crop applies to this scene only. The camera's crop in a preset scene still keeps its box's shape (a window over its unzoomed picture), as only the screen was asked to crop freely there.

**Over a scene, the screen's inset and Recenter are that scene's too.** A scene shows the recording's padded picture in its box, so the recording's inset would only pad what the scene had cropped away. A scene pads its own box instead (`RecordingSceneClip.inset`, a share of the box's shorter side, the same four looks): the box grows outwards by it and the picture is shown only in the box, the rest filled with the colour found behind the recording's content, as the recording's inset pads its crop (`Placement.inset`, `scene_window` on the output, which `output_placement` cuts the source crop to, so preview and export draw it alike). The padding eases in and out with the scene's arrival like its radius. A preset scene stays a preset: the padding is its box's whatever lays it out.

Select's handles and the press's hit target both lie on the padded box: `arrangedRecordingScene` pads the crop as `arrange.rs` does, keeping the bare box as `screen.box` for the gestures that edit it, so the handles a press draws are the ones left after it. A move or resize of a custom scene's screen is measured on the padded box and carried to the bare one (`movedPaddedSceneBox`, `SceneBox::moved_padded`).

Recenter over a scene looks for content in what the box shows (`sceneWindowSourceCrop`), crops the box to it and moves it, the picture with it, into the middle of where the box was (`recenteredScenePane`), the box then being the content alone with the scene's padding round it, as the recording's Recenter leaves its content in the middle of its padded crop; a preset is made custom first, as a crop makes it.

**A scene's crop only ever hides or shows the picture; it never scales it.** Cropping in place keeps the picture where it sits by zooming it relative to the box, and a scene zooms at most eight times (`MAX_SCENE_ZOOM`, `framing.rs`'s `MAX_ZOOM`) and keeps every box at least a fiftieth of the canvas (`MIN_BOX_SHARE`). Past either limit the framing or the box would be clamped while the window kept shrinking, and the picture would visibly shrink, so the window is held to those limits before it becomes the box (`heldToLimits`): the crop stops at the smallest window the picture can fill, grown back from the edge being pulled while the opposite edge stays put, and at the picture's edge it stops too.

**A ratio holds in recordings too, and a free crop can be locked.** The recording's crop windows carry `crop_keeps_shape` as a screenshot's do (`withCropShape`, `RecordingPreviewSelection.cropKeepsShape`): the screen's and the camera's, outside scenes and inside them, the screen's over a scene by that scene's own shape (`recordingCropAspectKey`), the camera's by its own. With a free crop drawn (not the whole capture; over a scene, a custom scene's box), the shapes line offers Locked as a word after Free (`free | locked | original | ratios`), like the other shapes rather than a glyph beside them; it stays offered while chosen. Locked (`CropAspect` `"locked"`), the crop is not cut, and the handles keep the shape the crop has, which a drag starts from, so no ratio of its own is recorded beside the choice; the choice is kept per layer and per scene in `crop-aspect-store.ts`, like the other shapes, for as long as the editor is open.

**A layer's inset is kept as a share.** The padding is stored as `inset`, a share of the layer's own picture's shorter side (`screenshot-inset.ts`), for screenshot layers and the recording's screen alike, as a scene's inset is. The frame (the crop rect) is still what is drawn; a new crop (`commitScreenshotCrop`), Recenter and a look picked grow it again by the share, so the padding keeps its proportion to the picture. The four looks are the shares 0, 0.03, 0.06 and 0.1 exactly (`INSET_LOOKS`). A project saved before has no `inset` (serde and TS default absent) and is read from its frame with the picture it loads with: half the frame's width past the picture over its shorter side, taken as the look whose rounded pixels it has where one matches, so it looks the same and a look picked then is still that look.

**The Frame island's dimensions scale the composition.** A typed width or height, and Reset to the source size, scale every layer with the canvas (`screenshot-canvas-scale.ts`): each layer's middle keeps its share of the canvas and the layer, its picture, padding and annotations, scale by the lesser of the two axes' scales, so it keeps its shape and never spills past a canvas narrowed in one axis. In a recording a camera drawn into the screen's canvas scales with it; scenes, held as shares, follow already. The change is one commit, one undo step. Dragging the frame's edges on the picture still leaves the layers where they are.

**Only what is seen is taken hold of.** An annotation is drawn with its own layer, so where a layer drawn above it (a screenshot layer, the recording's screen or camera, in the targets' back-to-front order) covers the press, the annotation is not picked: no choice, halo, cursor or pen grab there, and the press falls to the layer in front (`hidden_by_layer_above` in `screenwide_annotations::gesture`, used by `shaft_at_point`). The covering layer is its frame, rounded by its corners, read where each layer's turn draws it. The chosen annotation's grips, drawn as chrome over everything, stay grabbable.

**Annotations off the canvas show dimmed,** so they stay where they can be seen and taken hold of (user confirmed). **The crop dims only what it crops,** never the layers above it (user confirmed). **The paused preview stays sharp,** without the export's motion blur (user confirmed).

**A highlight past a crop's edge is a plain band in its colour.** Where a highlight runs off the picture (past what the crop leaves, `highlight_past_picture` in `annotation_highlight.wgsl`), it is drawn plain rather than recolouring the edge pixels the picture's fetch carries on, which drew stripes; inside the picture nothing changes. The preview and the export share the shader.

**A magnifier is one lens that enlarges what lies under its middle.** It is a box with a zoom (`AnnotationShape::Magnify { start, end, zoom }`): no separate zoom area, loupe or line between them. It is held by a box's eight grips, as a shape is, rounded by the corner looks, and enlarges by one of four looks, 1.5×, 2×, 3× and 4× (`MAGNIFY_ZOOMS`, `LENS_ZOOMS`, held equal by a Rust test), 2× for a fresh one; the next lens takes the last zoom set. The zoom is offered as glyphs on the Magnifier island's line, before the corners (size before shape). The lens shows the finished picture under it (background, padding, crop and corners as exported, so nothing the crop cut away shows), with the annotations drawn under it enlarged and those drawn over it not. Preview and export share the shader.

- **Mapping:** a lens shows the point `q` under it at `centre + (q - centre) × zoom`, so a press at `p` inside it lands on `centre + (p - centre) / zoom`. Lenses over lenses compose, bottom first. What lies under a lens's rim, past `1 / zoom` of the way out, shows nowhere (`magnify::lens`).
- **Hit priority:** a lens's own grips win; then anything drawn above the lens; then an annotation drawn under it, where it shows enlarged, which is chosen, gripped and dragged through the lens, the drag read back through it so what is carried follows the pointer where it shows; otherwise the press moves the lens. A shape's inside seen through a lens never takes the press, or a lens over a large shape could not be moved; a stroke is picked by its line alone, never its inside. Only annotations on the lens's own layer are seen through it. A box's frame and grips are drawn where its middle shows, so they stay one box; a disc grip where it shows; an image's frame and rings stay where the image lies.
- **Older projects:** a magnifier saved with a zoom area and a loupe loads as a lens over its zoom area's centre, as big as its loupe was, at the look nearest the zoom the loupe gave it (loupe size over the zoom area's longer side, nearest as a ratio, so 2.5× is 3×), in Rust (`lens_from_loupe`) and TypeScript (`magnifyShape`) alike. Its corners, dress and reveal carry over; it is saved back as a lens.

## The tool column and the title bar's modes

The tools for working on the picture stand in one column at the workspace's start edge (`toolbar/editor-tool-column.tsx`): Select, Crop and the pens. The modes, which open islands for the whole capture, are a centred group in the title bar (`title-bar/editor-mode-bar.tsx`), between the project's name and the window's commands; the preview publishes them there (`usePublishEditorModes`), as it does Copy Frame. This is the one exception to keeping tools out of the title bar.

- **Crop is a tool, not a mode:** it stands after Select, glyph alone, named in a tooltip with its key, C. In a recording, a press on the screen or the camera crops that layer, its handles on it (per scene inside a scene, the recording's own crop outside, see "A crop's shape"); taken up with no track chosen it takes the screen's. In a screenshot it crops the chosen layer. Its island holds only the crop: its shape (`CropAspectChoice`), the scene note over a scene, and Reset crop. Escape, Enter, Select, V and ⌘D put Select back, as from any tool. The Screen, Screenshot and Camera islands hold no crop rows, and opening them puts no crop handles on the picture.
- **Every pen:** image, arrow, text, redact, draw, shape, highlight, counter, focus and magnify, most used first. Spotlight is the focus pen's dimmed look, as on the annotate island, so it has no button.
- **A short window:** the column measures its height, and the pens that do not fit go into a More menu at the end of the pens; the pen last taken from it, by the menu or its shortcut, takes the place before the menu's button (`annotationToolLayout`).
- **Modes are named and drawn as their islands:** Frame, Screen (Screenshot for a screenshot), Camera, Scene; Cursor, Keyboard; Captions, Transcript, Sound, each group set apart by a gap. Which are offered follows what the export holds (below). A screenshot offers Frame and Screenshot; a recording of the camera alone offers Camera where Screen would stand (below); audio, with no picture to show captions over, offers Transcript and Sound, and its column stands empty. The Cursor island's glyph is the column's pointer with its click, since Select already has the bare pointer.
- **Tools are glyphs alone:** a mode button, like the column's tools, never shows its name; it names itself to assistive technology and, with its key, in a tooltip, and is filled while its mode is open. Pressing it opens the mode, pressing it again or Escape closes it. Text commands (Export, Copy Frame) keep their leading glyph and name. Short of room, the project's name truncates first; the modes and the window's commands keep their width. The group stands centred while the name and the commands leave it room.
- **Tracks is in the title bar too:** a recording's Tracks button stands at the end of the modes, a gap apart since it is not a mode, glyph alone, and opens or puts away the Tracks island; the strip's side column keeps play and the scissors.
- **Tooltips:** the column and the modes are a window toolbar, not an island, so their buttons name themselves and their letter in a tooltip.

## Which modes are offered

A mode is in the title bar only while it has something to act on in what the export holds now: what the recording was made with (`RecordedParts`), less the tracks switched off in Tracks (`captureParts`), and where the transcript stands. It is worked out again as any of them changes (`editorModeGroups`, worked out in `useOfferedModes` and published by the preview through `usePublishEditorModes`), and a mode's key does nothing while its mode is not offered. A group left with no mode drops with its gap.

| Frame | a picture is in the export: the recording's own (screen, or a camera-only recording's camera) or a camera beside it | audio-only; every video track switched off |
| Screen | the recording's screen is in | audio-only; the screen switched off; a camera-only recording, whose picture is the camera and worked on by Camera |
| Camera | a camera is in: one beside the screen, recorded and in the export, or the camera a camera-only recording is of, its own picture | no camera; the camera switched off |
| Scene | the recording's own picture is in, alone too: a scene zooms and moves the picture by itself, and the zooms planned from the cursor and keys are scenes, so it does not wait for a second track | audio-only; the screen switched off (a scene needs the picture it frames) |
| Cursor | cursor movement was recorded and the screen is in | no cursor data; the screen switched off; a camera-only recording |
| Keyboard | key presses were recorded and the screen is in | no key presses; the screen switched off; a camera-only recording |
| Captions | a picture is in, the microphone is in, and the transcript is not made-with-nothing-heard | no picture; no microphone or it switched off; transcribed and no words heard |
| Transcript | the microphone is in, and the transcript is not made-with-nothing-heard | no microphone or it switched off; transcribed and no words heard |
| Sound | any audio track is in the export | no audio; every audio track switched off |
| Tracks | the recording has two tracks or more to switch (picture, camera, each audio stream) | one track: there is nothing to choose, and the last track in is never left out |

- **The transcript still to make shows its modes:** with no model, while transcribing, after a failure, or before it is begun, Captions and Transcript are offered, since the Script island is where the model is fetched and the progress shown. Once made with no words, both go; there is no island saying nothing was heard.
- **Only the microphone is transcribed:** system audio alone offers Sound but not Captions or Transcript.
- **A screenshot always offers Frame and Screenshot:** it always has a layer to frame and dress.
- **The open mode goes with its button:** when the mode open stops being offered (the screen switched off under Cursor, say), it is put away as its button would (`useOfferedModes`): Select back in hand, nothing chosen, and its island leaves.
- **Coming and going:** each mode, gap and Tracks grows in from nothing and shrinks back out, fading, over `motionDurations.rearrange`, so the rest slide and the group stays centred as its width changes; at once with reduced motion, and never on the window's first render.

## Which island holds each setting

In the editor, the screen, camera and audio tracks have no panel of their own: their settings sit on the islands they belong with. The rule: a setting that holds for the whole recording sits on the island for its layer, and the Scene island keeps only what one scene sets over its stretch.

- **The Scene island keeps where each pane sits in a scene:** its layout, and each pane's zoom and position. With no scene in hand it frames the recording. A pane's corners, its turn and the camera following the face are its layer's, so Scene offers none of them; nor a shadow, which a scene has none of. Recenter for a followed face is Position's reset: while the camera follows the face, the middle of its box is the face.
- **The Frame island is the canvas alone:** its size and background, and for a screenshot the canvas's corners (`backgroundRadiusPercent`). These are the finished picture's outer corners; the Screen island's are the captured picture's, inside the padding.
- **Corners are set by the islands' three looks alone:** no layer, frame or annotation has a radius handle on the picture. A dragged radius would be a state the island could neither show nor give back (`CornerChoice` picks no look for it), and the handle would crowd the grips at every chosen corner. A project saved with a dragged radius keeps it until a look is picked.
- **The Screen island holds the screen of a recording or a screenshot, and is titled Screen or Screenshot after what was captured:** the inset presets (None, Tight, Medium, Large, shares of the picture's shorter side), which extend the edges with the colour found behind the content, then the screen's own corners and its drop shadow; then how the screen is turned (Rotation), for the whole recording or the whole screenshot layer; Recenter at its foot, putting what was captured back in the middle. Its crop is the Crop tool's. The Screen mode is the screen's track chosen with Select in hand, so the screen is moved and sized as Select moves it, and choosing the screen with Select opens it; it has no key, C being the Crop tool's.
- **The Camera island holds the camera, for the whole recording:** its effects (Enhance, Remove background, Blur or Replace drawn as icons with Replace at that line's end, and the objects picked in the matte), its corners and drop shadow, and whether it follows the face. Its crop is the Crop tool's. It opens from a Camera mode in the title bar, shown where the recording has a camera, or by choosing the camera with Select, the mode being the camera's track chosen with Select in hand. It is not part of Scene, because these apply to the whole recording and a scene to one stretch.
- **Over a scene, the Screen island's inset, corners, turn and Recenter, the Camera island's corners, the Camera island's Follow face, and the Crop tool's crop, are that scene's.** A scene can crop, pad, round and turn the screen, crop and round the camera and have the camera follow the face its own way, and the whole recording's setting would visibly do nothing under it. So with the playhead in a scene (`isInScene`), those controls edit the scene's own (its screen box and framing, see "A crop's shape"; `RecordingSceneClip.inset`, `radius`, its rotation, `followFace`), and a note at the island's foot says they apply to this scene only. Everything else on those islands holds for the whole recording. These are the only controls for a scene's own, and a scene that has its own keeps it: nothing hands it back the recording's. The recording's Follow face and a scene's are one control.
- **Show cursor is the recording's, and over a scene that scene's.** The Cursor island's row shows or hides the cursor (`CursorEffectSettings.bake`), drawn as the Keyboard island's Show shortcuts is, since some recordings (a game) want no cursor.
  - **Over a scene:** it is the scene's own (`RecordingSceneClip.cursor`), as the Screen and Camera islands set a scene's own; so the cursor can be off for the recording and on for a scene, or the other way round. The cursor fades in and out with the scene's arrival and leaving (`cursor_in_scenes`, on the scene clock), in the preview and the export, which draws the cursor wherever any part shows it. Unlike the others, a scene set back to the recording's own choice has none of its own again, so a later change to the recording reaches it.
  - **The options:** they show only where the cursor shows at the playhead, folding away with the eye (user chose, 2026-11-05), though they dress it wherever it shows.
  - **The note:** over a scene the note at the foot speaks only of the eye, since the options hold for the whole recording, and briefly, under the island's title (user asked, 2026-11-05): Hidden or Shown in this scene only where the scene makes its own choice, else Applies to this scene only (`CursorSceneNote`); alone, it sets its own width.
- **A camera-only recording's camera is the Camera mode, with the whole Camera island.** Its camera is the recording's own picture (the primary track), and worked on as a screen it would leave the camera's own tools out of reach.
  - **The mode:** the title bar offers Camera there in place of Screen, drawn and named as Camera, with no key, as Screen and Camera have none; choosing the picture with Select opens it, and it chooses the primary track (`columnMode`, `modeTool`, given the recording's `picture`).
  - **The island:** the Camera island with the camera's effects (Enhance, Remove background, Blur or Replace, picked objects), its corners and shadow, and what the Screen island gives a picture that still applies folded in where it belongs: the inset looks beside the corners, Rotation after the effects' lines, Recenter before Reset placement at the foot (`CameraIslandProps.picture`). Its crop stays the Crop tool's.
  - **No Follow face:** it holds the face in a camera box drawn into the screen's picture (the overlay, or a scene's camera pane), and a camera-only recording has neither, so it would do nothing.
  - **Over a scene:** the inset, corners, turn, Recenter and Reset placement are the scene's screen pane's, which is this camera, and the note at the foot says so in words without "screen".
  - **The effects:** they follow the primary track for such a recording, live in the preview, in stills, thumbnails and export (`CameraRecording::of`, `source_is_camera`, `camera_is_primary`).
- **Reset placement puts a layer back where a fresh project places it.** On the Screen island (the recording's screen, a screenshot's layer) and the Camera island (a camera beside the screen, or a camera-only recording's camera), at the end of the actions row at the island's foot, after Recenter where there is one: Recenter puts the picture back inside the layer, Reset placement then puts the layer back on the canvas.
  - **Its name:** "Reset placement", not "Reset", since the island holds other things a bare Reset could mean (inset, corners, rotation), and not "Reset size", since it puts the position back too.
  - **Where it puts the layer:** a fresh project fits the whole picture in its box about the box's middle: the canvas for a screen or screenshot layer, the corner `defaultCameraOverlay` gives a camera drawn into the screen's picture (on the canvas as it is now, so a canvas resized since places it as a fresh one of that size would). The reset fits what the layer shows now the same way, the picture behind its frame moving and scaling with it (`layer-placement.ts`): a layer never cropped lands exactly where a fresh project put it, and a cropped or padded one keeps its crop, padding, corners, shadow and turn, its frame filling the box as the whole picture would.
  - **What it leaves alone:** Rotation has its own reset on its row (Reset rotation, `IslandRotation`).
  - **As a change:** one change, one undo step, tweened as Recenter is. It is disabled, as the islands' other one-shot actions with nothing to do are (Captions' Reset), while the layer is at its default placement (`isAtDefaultPlacement`, within half a pixel).
- **Over a scene, Reset placement is the scene's pane's.** A preset scene's panes are where its layout puts them already (a first move makes a scene custom), so the action is disabled there. A custom scene's pane goes back in the box the scene's preset gives it, fitted at its own shape about that box's middle, so a pane cropped in place keeps its crop and framing, and one only moved and sized lands in the preset's box exactly; a full scene's panes go back where the recording's own composition puts them (`scenePaneAtLayout`, `ToolPanelScenePane.isAtLayout`). The scene stays custom; choosing its preset again on the Scene island still puts every pane back at once.
- **The Sound island holds every track's volume and the microphone's tools.** It opens from a Sound mode in the toolbar, also offered for audio-only recordings. Each track's volume is a typed figure in decibels, named by the track, with its reset; under the microphone's, its tools on one line: Studio sound, Reduce noise, Vocal cleanup, Auto volume and, while Auto volume is on and the recording has system audio, Studio ducking. Studio sound stands in for Reduce noise and Vocal cleanup while it is on. A tool whose model is missing offers its download in place of its toggle, as the Camera island's do. Remove silences stays with the pauses on the strip and with Transcript.

## The recording editor on islands

The recording editor has the tool column beside its picture and its modes in the title bar (`recording/preview/use-recording-column.ts`, mapping in `recording/recording-column.ts`, the twin of `screenshot-column.ts`). Both editors work through the column, the title bar's modes, the islands and, for recordings, the strip.

- **Modes and the picture's tool:** Frame is the frame drag (`canvas`), Scene is the scene tool. Screen, Camera, Cursor, Keyboard, Captions, Transcript and Sound are only an island, with Select kept in hand under them; Screen and Camera are their track chosen with Select, so choosing the screen or the camera opens its mode and choosing the other switches (see "A layer chosen with Select opens its mode"). Any of the picture's tools taken up (a pen, Crop, Frame, Scene) puts them away. A mode's key (F, L, Shift+C, K, U, Shift+T) opens it as its button does; Escape, Enter after a crop, Select and V put Select back with nothing chosen.
- **What is offered:** the title bar offers a mode only while the export holds something for it to work on (`editorModeGroups`, the table under "Which modes are offered"). Audio-only recordings show Transcript and Sound and no pens.
- **No Select panel:** a layer's settings are on the Screen, Camera and Sound islands. Choosing a caption or a shortcut on the strip opens the Captions or Keyboard mode.
- **Captions and Script at opposite corners:** the island window holds two islands (`host/placed-island.tsx`): the mode's in the picture's top end corner, and the Script island in its top start corner, mirrored in a right-to-left language. The Script stands there in the Transcript mode too, alone, so moving between Transcript and Captions never moves it: only the Captions island comes and goes at the end. Side by side, the Script would move aside as Captions opened and jump back as Captions tucked away; apart, neither waits on or moves for the other. The island window lies over the picture alone, so the start corner is the picture's, `EDGE_PX` in, clear of the tool column.
- **The pair never trades places:** Captions always shows both, whatever the transcript's state. Until its words are in (transcribing, no model, failed), the Script island is the Transcript mode's own, saying how the transcript is coming or offering the model, and the Captions island is only its row, so the state is said once, on the Script. Were Captions to drop the Script there and stand alone in the corner with the same line, moving between Transcript and Captions would read as the two islands swapping. The pair comes out together (`useSnapshotIsland` holds both until both are ready).
- **An editor island shrinks to nothing:** putting one away (a mode closed, a pen put down, a strip item let go, the Captions island leaving the pair) shrinks it to nothing rather than to the 44 by 10 speck the recording island shrinks to without a notch, which would stand on the picture a moment before it faded and read as a pill left behind (`shrinksToNothing` on `Island`, set by `EditorIsland`). The recording island still tucks into the notch, or to its speck.
- **Transcribing starts on its own:** the islands have no Transcribe before there is a transcript, so opening Captions or Transcript with the model on this computer starts it, once per recording.
- **The Scene island frames either pane** whichever track is in hand: the snapshot publishes each pane's framing (`ToolPanelScene.panes`) and `sceneFramingPane` names the pane a framing change reaches. With no scene under the playhead it shows the recording's own boxes as Custom, and choosing a layout adds a scene there. Rotation is the Screen island's.
- **A scene is drawn the same on the island and the strip:** the Scene island's row glyph and layout tiles and the strip's scene rail draw one `SceneLayoutGlyph`, the strip's from `sceneClipDrawing` (the scene's own boxes, a full scene's recording boxes, else its preset, on the video's real shape), so a scene reads the same in both. The screen pane is drawn turned as the scene turns it, in every tile, since a layout chosen keeps the turn: a CSS perspective turn about the pane's middle, seen from twice the video's longer side, which matches `turned_homography` in `rotation.rs`. The camera stays upright, as in the picture.
- **Sound holds every track:** the snapshot publishes all tracks (`soundTracks`), `trackVolume` sets any one's level, and the microphone's tools are the recording's microphone's whichever track is selected.
- **Camera output lives in the Export island:** whether the camera is drawn into the video or saved as a file of its own is a choice about the finished files, so it is an option toggle on the Export island, shown while the recording keeps both its screen and its camera. The islands only follow it: the Scene island places the camera only while it is drawn in. The choice travels as `cameraOutput` in the export patch to the editor's `bakeCamera`.
- **A camera with its background removed stays inside its box:** the person is cut by the camera box's edges like any other camera picture, so a body ends at the box's bottom edge; nothing of the person spills past the box (user confirmed). MP4 has no transparency, so a separate camera file whose background is taken away is filled with a key colour another editor keys out: green (`#00b140`) or blue (`#0047bb`, for someone wearing green), chosen on the Export island (`CameraKey`).
- **Shortcuts chosen on the strip:** choosing shortcuts in the strip's shortcut lane opens the Keyboard mode and keeps them chosen under it; the Keyboard island says how many and deletes them, asking twice (`ToolPanelSnapshot.chosenShortcuts`, `deleteChosenShortcuts`). Delete in the lane deletes them too.

## The strip's islands and the Tracks island

- **Where a strip item's island stands:** a beat, lull, moment or range chosen on the strip brings out its island at the picture's foot, centred over the item's x (kept over the picture), following the strip as it scrolls. The chosen item's element carries `data-strip-island`; where several carry it (gathered items, or one item's parts either side of a jump) the island centres over them all.
- **One island at a time:** `recording/preview/use-recording-island.ts` decides between the island of the column's tool or the title bar's mode, the strip item's and the Tracks island; whichever was taken up last is out, the others put away. Escape lets go of the strip choice (the strip's own deselect) or the Tracks island.
- **What travels:** the preview publishes the strip choice through `strip-island-channel.ts` (`ToolPanelSnapshot.stripItem`, and `chosenShortcuts` for the shortcut lane) and the asks come back as `cutStripItem`, `lullPlay`, `trimLullToBreath`, `deleteChosenShortcuts`; tracks as `ToolPanelSnapshot.tracks` and `trackEnabled`.
- **Several beats and lulls gathered with Shift** bring out the strip group's island (`stripGroup`, `island/strip-group/`), as the Marquee island does for annotations: how many, and Cut, asking twice, which cuts them all as Delete does. One alone shows its own island.
- **Voice notes play in the island window** (`host/use-moment-note.ts`), and the Transcripts model is downloaded from there, not through a patch: neither is editor state.
- **Tracks island** opens from the title bar's Tracks button and stands in the picture's top end corner, as every other island does.
- **An island tucking away stays where it stood:** `PlacedIsland` keeps the last place it was given (`at`) until it has hidden, rather than moving as it leaves.
- **An island moving to another place shrinks away and comes out again there:** another strip item chosen elsewhere, or a corner's island giving way to a strip item's or back, puts the island away where it stood and brings it out where it is going (`PlacedIsland`), rather than flying across the picture with what it held; the same item moving as the strip scrolls is followed at once. The item's place and its island reach the island window in separate messages, the place first, so the place carries the item it was measured over (`PopupPanelIslandContent.anchor`) and the island keeps its old place until its content is that item's (`editor-island-panel.tsx`); without that, the old item's island would jump to the new item before giving way.

## Pinning an annotation to its content

- **A pin toggle on every pinnable annotation's island:** a recording's screen annotations (`isPinnable`) carry a pin at the row's end, before Animate (options come before animation), drawn outlined while free and filled while pinned (`island/annotation/annotation-pin.tsx`). Turning it on is the menu's Pin to Content: pinned at the playhead where it falls in the clip, else where the annotation was placed; off unpins. A screenshot's, the camera's and a pen's next one have no pin (`ToolPanelAnnotation.pin` absent).
- **How tracking stands, at the island's foot:** while pinned, a note before the one-shot actions says what holds at the playhead: Tracking the content (`IslandWorkLine`, with the tracker's progress) until the path has landed, else Following the content, or Content out of view here where the annotation is hidden there (by the tracker or by the hand). Under it, the menu's answer for the playhead (Out of view / Back in view, each offered only where the menu offers it) and Clear corrections when there are any, as buttons at the end of their own row that wrap rather than widen the island (pseudo-locale checked).
- **Where tracking is unsure, the note warns:** at the playhead inside the clip, where the tracker lost the content (`weak`), found it off the frame (`hidden`) or under a cover (`under`), the note becomes a warning caption with a small warning icon (`Alert` `color="warning"`): Tracking lost the content here, Content off the frame here, or Content under a cover here (`pinUnsureAt`, lost first, as it says least about where the content went). It takes the place of the Following or out-of-view note, and the answers under it stay as the menu offers them, so an unsure place is checked and put right with the same Out of view / Back in view, a drag on the picture, or Clear corrections. The strip draws the same stretches in the warning colour on the bar. The kinds are told apart here rather than on the bar, as a caption has room to say which.
- **One pin vocabulary:** the island's asks are the menu's Tracking actions (`AnnotationPinAction`), sent as `annotationPin` and done by `actOnPin` against the same `recordingAnnotationPinning`, so the right-click Tracking section stays the quick path and both act alike. The view is `recordingPinView` (`recording-annotation-pin-view.ts`), from `useRecordingPinStatus` and the menu's `canHideHere`/`canShowHere`.
- **Following the playhead without rendering per frame:** how a pin stands depends on the playhead, which lives outside React; `useRecordingAnnotationPins` reads it through `useSyncExternalStore` as text, so the editor renders only when what the pins show changes (crossing into or out of a stretch out of view or unsure), not on every frame played.
- **No pin mark on the picture's selection chrome:** the native chrome draws only quads, discs and hairlines, and the workspace's editing engine has no notion of a pin; a mark would need a pinned flag carried from the recording's annotation sync into the workspace state and a glyph drawn in the chrome's vertices. Not cheap, so skipped: the island's filled pin and the strip's bar say it instead.

## The picture moving to an island's setting

- **The first frame handed on is the old look:** the native preview is handed the drawn settings from passive effects (`use-screenshot-preview-surface.ts`, the recording layout, `use-scene-preview-sync.ts`), which React runs before the tween's first step re-renders. Returning the new settings on the render that brings them would draw the new look for a frame, then the old one, then animate. So the render that brings an asked change still returns what was drawn (`pictureTweenAsked` reads the ask without using it), and `useTweenedOutput` starts the tween from there. Outside a tween the hook holds no state, so a target built afresh each render settles (`use-tweened-output.test.tsx`).
- **One ask, each part takes it:** `askPictureTween(workspace)` marks the next change. The picture's settings (`part: "picture"`) and a recording's scenes (`part: "scenes"`) each take it for themselves, because a setting chosen inside a scene changes the scenes and one outside it changes the recording's settings.
- **Preview only:** only what is drawn passes through the in-between settings (`picture-between.ts`, `scene-clips-between.ts`). The project and the export get the new settings straight away, and the tween's last step is exactly those settings.

## Your caption templates' failures

Importing, exporting and removing one of your caption templates (`text_template_files.rs`) has no surface in the editor: nothing calls the commands, and they show no failure themselves. Each rejects with a whole translated sentence, the failure then the English reason (`editor-template-import-failed` and its siblings), for whichever surface offers them to show as an error `Alert` line: the Text island's template picker (`IslandAside`), or failing that the editor island's foot.

## The Export island

- **An island, not a window:** the title bar's Export button brings out the Export island at the picture's top end corner, where every island stands (`island/export/`, kind `"export"`). It stands in for any other island while it is out, and is the one island `isLocked` does not put away (`use-snapshot-island.tsx`). Whether it is out lives in the editor window's store (`export/export-island-store.ts`), which `useEditorIslandWindow` reads.
- **What it holds:** the file line, first in the section and flush with its inset like every other part, so the island lines up the same whether or not a compare or the looks widen it (its name in a framed field, `TextField` `isFramed`, so it reads as typed into; its folder as a `PathField`, which shows no tooltip on an island, the path in its accessible name; its format at the line's end as pills, PNG, JPEG or WebP for a screenshot, and for a recording its video (MP4, or the recording's own container where it cannot be copied) or its sound alone as M4A or WAV where it has any, the sound alone only when nothing of the picture is exported (`recording-format.ts`)); the compare (below); four looks, Smallest, Smaller, Balanced and Original (`export-looks.ts`, below); the options that apply; and a plain Export. Presets only: there is no Customise, and settings that match no look, as an older export may have left them, take Balanced as the island comes out. A screenshot or audio has no looks: a screenshot keeps its own format choice, and audio is offered no quality at all (`editor-export.ts` publishes it for a video only).
- **The looks are four cards:** four equal cards in one row, smallest first, a React Aria radio group moved through with the arrow keys and chosen by a press anywhere on a card (`export-look-choice.tsx`). Each card, on the base fill with comfortable padding and its content centred, holds the look's name and the picture's size under it as a caption, both known at once (user asked for centred, 2026-11-07). No badges. The chosen card stands on the `selected` surface, and a hovered card takes a step up in fill. A long name wraps inside its card (it is sized by the card, `contain-inline-size`) rather than widening the island; the figures never wrap.
- **Options are words with switches, the island's exception to glyph toggles:** an export cannot be undone and its options cannot be previewed, so each is a `ControlRow` saying what it does, with a `Switch` (subtitles, camera as its own file, mixing the audio tracks, showing the file when done, moving the project to Recently Deleted or the Recycle Bin after). A choice an option brings is a row of pills on the option's own line, before its switch, while the option is on (SRT or WebVTT; Green or Blue once the camera's background is taken away), named for assistive technology; the pills are a control's height, so the row never grows as they come (`IslandParts`).
- **No file size before the export:** the cards once showed each look's estimated file size, measured by encoding samples of the recording at every look. On a 10-minute 3.5K recording that took about 10 CPU cores for 7 to 10 seconds and nearly 1 GB of memory each time the island came out, and across 44 real exports it was off by more than 20% for nearly a quarter of them, up to 38%. Cheaper guesses from the source's size, a cap or the scrub strip's pictures were three to eight times worse, since the size follows how much moves on screen, which none of them see. The compare shows what a look trades away for about a second of work per change, so the island shows that and the file's real size once it is written (user decided, 2026-11-07).
- **Closing it:** before the export, Escape, the title bar's Export button or a press anywhere off the island puts it away: the island's window takes every press over the editor's content while it is out (`editor-island-panel.tsx`), and the editor window every press round it (`use-export-light-dismiss.ts`), so the press reaches nothing under it; the Export button is left to toggle. With GIF chosen, the island window lets presses through and the strip and the Range tool (`data-gif-range`) keep them, so drawing the GIF's range leaves it out (user asked, 2026-11-04). A hint or a Cancel button would only repeat that, so there is none. While it exports a press off it does nothing; only Cancel export stops it. Done, a press off it lets the finish go.
- **It reaches past the picture:** the Export island is taller than most pictures leave room for. It stands in the picture's top end corner as every island does, and reaches down past the picture's foot over the strip and the tool column, which are set back and locked while it is out anyway, as far as the island window, which covers the whole content area. Where even that is too short, the compare, looks and options scroll between the file line and Export, which stay in view (`use-export-body-room.ts`, from the room the window leaves, `island-room.ts`). The scroll stops at its ends rather than springing past them (`ScrollArea` `overscroll="none"`): the window can leave it only a few px short, and a spring larger than all there is to scroll reads as the content jumping.
- **The editor locks while it stands over it:** open, exporting or showing its finish, the tool column, the title bar's modes and the strip are set back and blurred, the picture's gestures stand down, and only playing and the playhead still answer, with Space, Escape and ⌘E (`export-island-keys.ts`); the strip's playhead takes the pointer only while it exports, since before and after a press on the strip puts the island away as one on the picture does.
- **Exporting and the finish:** the island turns into a work line with Cancel export, a secondary button that asks twice (Stop and discard); Escape or the Export button tucks it away, and the title bar's button shows a progress ring. Done, it shows the file (Reveal, Copy, and a chip dragged out as the file); after 8 seconds, Escape or a press off it, it shrinks away and the editor stays open on the project, to be worked on and exported again (user asked, 2026-11-03). Only a project moved to Recently Deleted with the export takes the editor with it, once the island is put away (`put_away_export`); until then the window keeps showing it under the finish rather than going blank. There is no Share: the drag and Copy already hand the file on. Copy and the drag are macOS only and left out on Windows. A failure brings the island back with an error line, the island's alert (a caption with its icon on the text column), and Try again at the line's end.
- **Copy is not an export:** a screenshot's Copy (⌘C, Ctrl+C) puts the picture on the clipboard and closes the editor, since taking a screenshot, annotating it and copying it is the usual flow (`copy_editor_to_clipboard`). A recording's Copy Frame copies only the frame under the playhead and leaves the editor open (`copy_recording_preview_frame_to_clipboard`). User confirmed, 2026-11-03.
- **Formats are pills on the file line:** a screenshot offers PNG, JPEG and WebP; a recording its video (MP4, or MOV where FFmpeg is missing) and its sound alone as M4A or WAV where it has sound (`recordingFormatChoice`). Choosing the sound alone retitles the island Export audio and leaves out what is about the picture: the compare, the looks and the separate camera, and no compared frame is made meanwhile (`useExportMeasures`). A WAV holds one stream, so its tracks are always mixed and the mix switch is hidden; it is 16-bit PCM, as the recording's own sound is AAC. The choice holds for one recording; the next starts out as its video (`useRecordingFormat`).
- **The compare:** under the file line, the frame at the playhead as drawn, never encoded, on the start side of a divider, and as the chosen look's encoder leaves it on the end side, pixel for pixel, the picture dragged to look elsewhere and the divider a slider (`export-compare.tsx`, `preview_recording_export_frame`, made by the editor in `use-export-compare.ts` and published in the snapshot). Its Original and Export badges stand in every state, on the island's black so they read over any picture. Choosing a look makes only the exported frame again; a moved playhead both, each debounced; while they are made the picture softens under a spinner. A JPEG or WebP screenshot is compared with its picture as drawn (`preview_screenshot_export`); a PNG is written as drawn and has none.
- **Closing reshapes as opening does:** the Export island stands in for another by its kind (`useEditorIslandWindow`), and every other island is put away only while an export runs, read from the export under way rather than the lock, which lifts a snapshot later; read from the lock, the island coming back would be put away for a moment and brought out again, snapping instead of reshaping.
